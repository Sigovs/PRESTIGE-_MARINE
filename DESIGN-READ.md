## 2. Concept

> **A Prestige Marine boat is the car client's next object, so the page reads boats the way that client already reads cars — from straight above, as one figure on a ground — and the only thing that has changed is the ground.**

**Concept Gate: PASS**
- CG1: the sentence opens on the buyer and the business, not on a style or a device.
- CG2: one sentence, and it could be wrong. If these buyers buy boats as lifestyle rather than as an object, the page is wrong.
- CG3: swap the overhead view for deck-lifestyle shots and the sentence collapses. The technique carries the meaning.
- CG4: stripped static, it is still a page of figures-from-above on a sand ground: a hull painted in a Lamborghini colour, then six marques, then a car. It still makes its point.
- CG5: none of the sibling builds uses an overhead camera or a light ground. The light ground is required by this project's footer, not inherited.
- CG7: every section carries the sentence; see the ledger in §9. The weakest carriers are Inventory and Contact, and both are named there.

**Why this sentence.** The flagship stock boat is the proof, and it was already in the inventory folder. It is a **2026 Midnight Express 52 Vitesse, custom painted in "Lamborghini Grigio Lynx with Camel seating"** (listing, verbatim). The research has a straight-down drone frame of exactly that boat (`inventory/…10173015/14.jpg`). Seen from above, the hull is a grey figure on a ground, which is how a Lamborghini reads from a drone. Two of the six marques carry the Lamborghini name: Tecnomar for Lamborghini 63 and the SEABOB SE63 for Automobili Lamborghini. Brett David's founding line on About says the same thing in words: the link between "clients that owned premium exotic automobiles and the luxury lifestyle of the yachting industry".

**Page grammar (DNA36):** *continuous world.* There is one camera altitude throughout. The ground changes beneath it: water, then shore sand, then road for one chapter, then sand, then surf.

## 4. Composition plan (masses before components)

Six masses:
1. **Water stage + H1**
2. **Hull column with its words**
3. **Brand index** (a type column and a sticky frame)
4. **The Land chapter** (one car from above plus the founding line)
5. **Inventory + Sold + Contact** (the working mass)
6. **The surf**

- **Compositional centre:** the page's vertical axis. **The axis belongs to the hull** in Acts I–II: nothing persistent sits on it (see the header in §7). From Act III the centre of action moves right of the axis, into the brand frame. The type holds the left, so the hull's vertical is answered by long horizontal lines of type: tension with counterweight (C10).
- **Eye path:** H1 → bow tip at the bottom edge → down the hull, one fact per part → stern plate (price, CTA) → brand names → car → stock → the sea.
- **Rhythm of asks:** nothing → read/desire → browse → read → browse/act → act → nothing. No two adjacent acts make the same demand.

## 5. Peak and signature

**THE PEAK: Act II, "The passage".** It gets the asset budget (the only bespoke cut-out and the hero loop) and the most scroll room (2.4 vh). The silence before it is the hero itself: one headline and nothing else. It is the page's only scroll-bound device that consumes the visitor's advance.

**THE SIGNATURE MOVE: the stock boat is the cursor.** A tall transparent cut-out of *the actual Midnight Express 52 Vitesse on sale*, bow up, rides up the page at plain document speed:
- It rises out of a fixed top-down water loop and passes through the headline, which fades as the bow crosses it.
- It crosses the line where the water dissolves into sand and runs the length of four short facts about its own parts, set left and right of the hull at the height of the part they describe.
- Its stern lands on the price.

Why this is more than AB Yachts' device with new parameters:
- The figure is a listing, not a brand render.
- The words annotate the hull they sit beside.
- The paint is a Lamborghini colour, which is the whole thesis.
- When the boat sells, the stern plate turns to **Sold** and the passage becomes track record (C17 dependency, declared below).

**VERY SIMPLE, as Alex insisted.** No canvas, no frame sequence and no pin. The water stage is CSS-sticky; the hull and the words are in normal flow. There is exactly one scroll-linked property: the H1's opacity, plus a few px of rise, as the bow crosses it. No blur: blur over video is a frame-rate tax for an effect opacity already gives. The words arrive once, by opacity only (≈240 ms, no travel).

**Declared dependency (C17).** The passage depends on one listing photo.
- Authored constraint: the figure must be a straight-down frame of a represented boat, bow up, on a hull that holds an edge on pale sand.
- Operational risk: the boat sells, or the original never arrives (§13).
- Survival: on sale, the plate flips to "Sold · [date]" and the facts stay true. If a better top-down of the next flagship arrives, it replaces the hull and its four facts as one unit.

### Does a scroll-scrubbed film (Omaya) also belong here? No.

Alex loved Omaya's "the page is animation and video" feeling; the answer below says how this page delivers it without a second scrubbed film.
- The scroll is the transport for the hull. A canvas frame sequence would be a second primary temporal idea driven by the same input. MJ2 is advice since 25 Oct, but DNA28 (one peak) and DNA37 (one signature, never a second instance) still decide it: two scroll-scrubbed acts make two peaks, so the page has none.
- No owned continuous film exists. Every brand film is someone else's cut, and 150–200 webp frames is 10–20 MB.
- **The removal test (MJ10).** Remove a scrubbed film and nothing about these boats becomes harder to understand. Remove the hull passage and the Lamborghini paint, the stabiliser and the price all leave with it.

**Omaya's feeling is kept by distributing motion, not by stacking it.** Every act has something moving, and never two band-1 ideas at once:
- the water loop under the H1;
- the hull's travel;
- one brand loop at a time in the index;
- the surf.

## 10. Palette and type direction

### Palette (DNA20)
It derives from the subject's material and light. The values are sampled by the designer from the named sources, not invented.

- **Ground:** dry pale sand, warm and low-chroma, light enough for ink at AAA. **Sampled from the generated surf loop's dry band**, because the footer join only works if the ground *is* that sand. The page stands on the beach it ends on.
- **Ink:** graphite **from the Grigio Lynx hull's shaded side**. It is not navy (Omaya's) and not black; it keeps the type the same family as the figure.
- **Accent:** **camel, from the ME52's seating.** It is used only as a fill behind ink type (CTA) and for focus rings. It is never text on sand, because it will fail AA there; the designer measures this on the composited render.
- **Water colours live only in footage**, never as tokens. One grade across all footage and photos: Miami midday, Biscayne green-teal (DNA23). Brand photos that fight the grade (dusk, studio) are not used as lead frames.
- **Why light, against the cinematic dark default (DNA22):** the surf footer must-have needs a light ground; shallow Biscayne water and sand are a light world; and a graphite hull holds figure-ground on pale sand, where it would sink on navy.

### Type
One variable family, two voices: **Archivo** (Google Fonts, `wdth` 62–125, `wght`).

- **Display** is set wide (≈112–125) and medium, in sentence case. A wide horizontal line of type answers the narrow vertical hull: tension with counterweight (C10). It also keeps long marque names ("Tecnomar for Lamborghini 63") as single strong bars in the index.
- **Text** is set at width 100, regular, 16–18 px on 60–75 ch.
- **Prices** use tabular figures, to be verified (I5).
- **No mono, no micro-labels, no italic signature word:** that is auction-editorial habit, and it is not named.
- The designer verifies the display face in the render at the sizes the page sets, on water and on sand. If Archivo's wide cut draws badly at H1 size over footage, the fallback is a different wide grotesque, chosen for the same reason, never a serif.

### The hull cut-out
- **Why the ME52 Vitesse, over the other candidates:**
  - It is **the real boat on sale**, so the passage ends on a real price.
  - Its paint is **Lamborghini Grigio Lynx**, which is the thesis.
  - It was shot on Miami water.
  - It is already **bow-up and near-nadir** (no rotation).
  - A graphite hull holds its edge on pale sand.
- **Losers:**
  - **Nyx:** an official image, not stock, and a white hull that dissolves into the sand ground.
  - **Pardo E72:** bow-left, terraces open, beam so wide that the cut-out is squat (≈1:2.3) with no length to carry four facts, and not a Prestige stock model.
  - **VanDutch 75:** teak and white on sand gives low figure-ground; it is enveloped in foam (a hard matte) and not in stock.
  - **The Pardo 43 Facebook shoot:** its only overhead (`fb-pardo43-02.jpg`) is 1440 px with the hull ≈250 px long, and the boat was a May listing.
- **Resolution:** the hull is ≈1,230 px tall in the 2100 px listing file. That is sharp only at DPR1 up to ≈1.35 vh at 900 px tall. **Ask the client or photographer for the drone original.**
- **No generative upscale of this hull** (GI3): it is the actual item for sale, and a model inventing its detail is a false statement about it. Masking may use a matting model only to compute the mask; the hull pixels stay the photographer's. Record this in a sidecar.
- **Shadow:** a separately authored soft sun shadow layer (DNA59), not baked in.
- **Lettering:** the transom name lettering ("B.A.D") is visible at full size. Keep it (it is the real boat), or crop to the cowls if the client prefers.

### The two water loops
Both are generated, which is acceptable under GI3: abstract water is a scene with no product, no premises and no person.
- **`gen-water-topdown`:** desktop 16:9 1920×1080 and mobile 9:16, 6–8 s seamless loop. Straight-down clear shallow green-teal water, slow ripple, sun glints. **No boat, no wake, no land, no foam, no text.** A calm, darker, even-value band in the upper-middle third for the H1 (GI5), and a lighter lower quarter that meets the sand dissolve.
- **`gen-surf-topdown`:** desktop 16:9 (composed for an ≈810 px-tall band) and mobile 4:5, 7–8 s seamless loop. No people, no footprints, no objects, no text.

## 15. Critique panel (on the direction)

1. **Composition.** The page's one idea fits in one sentence; the overhead figure strengthens it, and nothing on the page competes with it. Risk: Act III's sticky frame recentres the page right of axis immediately after the hull owned the centre. That is intended, but the seam II→III must read as a cut (the interruption shot), not a wobble.
2. **Craft.**
   - The H1 over moving water needs AA measured on the composited render across the whole loop (DM5), and the calm band must be generated, not scrimmed.
   - Camel fails AA as text on sand; it is a fill only.
   - 14 px floor on mobile hull facts: hence the single left column.
3. **User advocate.** Top jobs:
   - (a) call: one tap, header phone;
   - (b) see stock: one tap from the hero CTA or the header;
   - (c) find a brand: Menu or Act III.
   On the 50th visit, the 2.4 vh passage is long for a returning buyer. The header returns on scroll-up, and "View inventory" is in the hero.
4. **Brief advocate.** "Represent is one of the main parts": served with 2.6 vh, its own films and the Lamborghini pair first. "Live inventory matters": served by the price in the peak and Act V. But Act V sits fifth.
5. **Contrarian.** The strongest case against: the peak sells *one used boat*, and the day it sells, the homepage's best moment advertises a sale that already happened. The alternative most worth considering is a hull from a new-build marque (Tecnomar 63 from above) that never goes out of stock. It is rejected: no straight-down Tecnomar frame exists in research, and a sold flagship as track record is still a true, strong statement.
